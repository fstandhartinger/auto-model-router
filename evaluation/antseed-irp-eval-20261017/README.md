# AntSeed router evaluation, 17 Oct 2026 (60 public tasks, 6 models)

**Question.** On a fixed public prompt set, how much does routing with `auto_router.irp.rank()` save versus always using
the best model, at equal quality, and what does it cost versus always-cheapest? Prices are what an AntSeed buyer pays
today (snapshot of 17 Oct 2026, 10:27 UTC).

## Short answer (read this before quoting any number)

* **This task set does not separate the six models.** They pass 51 to 55 of 60 tasks (85.0 % to 91.7 %); 48 tasks are
  passed by all six and 4 by none. The 95 % intervals are about +/-10 points wide. The data cannot show a quality gap
  between Claude Opus 5 and a 10-100x cheaper model, and it cannot show the absence of one either.
* **Always-best is GPT-5.6 Luna** (55/60), the model that is also nearly the cheapest at AntSeed cheapest-seller prices
  (USD 0.0003 for all 60 tasks). Against it **the router saves nothing**: with the s1 classifier, tradeoff 0-4 costs 100-445 %
  of always-best's cost, tradeoff 5-10 costs 38-93 % at 3.3-6.7 points lower pass rate.
* **Against the conventional default, always-Claude-Opus-5** (54/60, USD 0.0176), the s1 router at tradeoff 2 costs
  **1.9 %** of Opus's cost (95 % CI 1.2-2.6 %) at 53/60 vs 54/60: pass-rate difference -1.7 points (95 % CI -6.7 to +3.3).
  Tradeoff 2-10 all cost 0.7-1.9 % of Opus's cost at 51-53/60. Part of this is price: the cheapest AntSeed sellers of Opus
  ask about USD 0.62 per million output tokens (OpenRouter list: 25), so the ratio depends heavily on which sellers are used
  (median-seller prices are in the sensitivity table, where the s1 router at tradeoff 2 costs 8.9 % of Opus).
* **The registered headline** (cheapest s1 setting that is not significantly worse than always-best): tradeoff 10 at
  **37.7 % of always-best's cost** (95 % CI 25.9-56.2 %), pass-rate difference -3.3 points (95 % CI -10.0 to +3.3). **But
  tradeoff 10 sends all 60 tasks to DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, so it is the same as always-cheapest and says nothing about
  routing skill.** No s1 setting is non-inferior within 5 points (lower CI bound >= -5), and none matches always-best's
  point estimate.
* **s1 vs prior only (no classifier, the router as deployed this morning).** At tradeoff 2-7 the classifier lowers cost to
  49-70 % of the prior-only router at equal or slightly better pass rate (tradeoff 2: +3.3 points, 95 % CI 0.0 to +8.3;
  cost ratio 0.68, CI 0.49-0.86). At tradeoff 0-1 the prior-only router sends everything to GPT-5.6 Sol (54/60) while s1
  mixes cheaper models (52/60) at 15-18 % of the cost (-3.3 points, CI -8.3 to 0.0). At tradeoff 8 the prior-only router is cheaper
  (all DeepSeek). The pass-rate differences are within noise, the cost differences are not.
* **The router never picked Claude Opus 5** (in none of its 2,640 recorded picks) and picked GPT-5.6 Sol 9 times with s1
  and 120 times prior-only (cheapest-seller prices), because at these prices and with its success model the cheaper models rank first. On this
  set that happened to be right (Opus is not better than the cheap models here); on harder tasks it may not be.

## Method

* **Tasks** (`tasks-public.jsonl`, 60, seeded sample, seed 20261017, fixed order, deterministic graders in `grade.py`):

  | Category | n | Sources (licence) | Check |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | coding | 15 | HumanEval+ x7, MBPP+ x8 (Apache-2.0) | the dataset's own hidden tests, run in the repo's bubblewrap sandbox with a nonce-verified verdict |
  | math | 15 | GSM8K x5 (MIT), MATH level 4-5 x5 (MIT), AIME 2024 x5 (MIT per its dataset card; the problems are (c) MAA) | exact final answer |
  | knowledge | 15 | MMLU-Pro, 10 options (MIT) | exact letter |
  | everyday | 15 | synthetic, generated by `build_tasks.py` (CC0): unit conversion, date arithmetic, JSON extraction, invoice/loan/calendar arithmetic | exact value or JSON equality |

  Difficulty labels (5 easy / 5 medium / 5 hard per category, except coding 5/6/4) are proxies: reference-solution length
  (coding), dataset (math), subject group (knowledge), construction (everyday). GPQA is not used (its licence asks that it not
  be republished). All keys are published: every source is public, and this is a router test, not a leaderboard.
  The set is disjoint from the tasks that calibrated the success model: `check_disjoint.py` compares by content against
  `experiments/tasks*.py`, the paired-study tasks, GSM8K rows 0-199, MATH-500 and AIME 2025 and finds no overlap; the
  HumanEval+ ids the earlier seeded build could reach are excluded. HumanEval/32 was excluded before any model ran because its
  dataset test fails the reference solution (found by `validate_graders.py`, which also confirms that every task passes with
  its reference/key and fails a stub).
* **Models** (all listed on AntSeed in the snapshot) and what was called: `anthropic/claude-opus-5`, `openai/gpt-5.6-sol`,
  `openai/gpt-5.6-luna`, `moonshotai/kimi-k3`, `z-ai/glm-5.3-flash`, `deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash` on OpenRouter, default
  reasoning settings, user message only, `max_tokens` 8000, one call per (task, model), transport errors retried at most twice.
  Truncated (8 of 360 answers, `finish_reason=length`), empty and errored answers fail. 360 of 360 calls returned an answer (no errors, no retries).
* **Prices** (`prices.json`): the cheapest AntSeed seller per model (lowest input+output price; zero-priced offers excluded) is
  the primary price, the median over sellers a sensitivity. An answer's cost is its real prompt/completion tokens (including
  reasoning tokens, as billed) times that price; prompts are unique so cached tokens are 0.
* **Router.** `auto_router.irp.rank()` called exactly as the whichmodel service calls it (candidates with caller prices,
  `max_tokens` 8000), on a router built as in `auto-router-demo` (`app/engine.py`: catalog entries, ModelInfo from Benchmark
  Heaven by `bench_id`, success model `app/data/success.measured.json`): (a) with `LocalS1Classifier` (pinned revision,
  digests in `router-catalog.json`; the run aborts if s1 falls back), (b) no classifier. Built from `auto-model-router` main
  at 973d60c; the parallel change to `irp.py` is not included. `kimi-k3` is not in the demo catalog and got the entry in
  `router_plan.py` (`kimi-k3::max`). The router's picks for every task, tradeoff 0..10, both classifiers and both
  price bases are in `router-plan.csv`, recorded **before any answer existed** and frozen by `manifest.json`.
* **Statistics** as registered in `analysis-plan.md`: Wilson 95 % intervals, paired bootstrap over tasks (10,000 draws, seed
  20261017) for pass-rate differences and cost ratios.

## Limitations (please keep them with any number)

1. **Ceiling effect and small n.** 48 of 60 tasks are passed by every model; differences of 1-4 tasks are noise. This set
   measures "does routing to cheap models lose quality on mostly easy tasks", not "does the router find the one task a
   cheap model fails". A harder set (e.g. more AIME, GPQA-style, long agentic tasks) is needed for the second question.
2. **Tasks nobody passes** (kept, as registered; paired differences are unaffected, the absolute rates are up to 4 tasks lower than on a clean set): `coding-mbpp-74`
   and `coding-mbpp-16` (MBPP+ tests encode conventions the prompt does not state; mbpp-16 is failed by 5 of 6),
   `knowledge-mmlupro-6904` (all six answer A, key G: an MMLU-Pro label error in our reading), `knowledge-mmlupro-5081`
   (five say H, key D, one truncated) and `everyday-11-invoice-json` (all six give 3.83 for 4.5 x 0.85 = 3.825, our key has
   3.82 from Python float rounding: our key defect). `tasks_failed_by_all_models` in `analysis.json` lists the four that no
   model passed (mbpp-74, 6904, 5081, everyday-11).
3. **Selection.** The headline picks the cheapest of 11 settings on the same 60 tasks the settings are judged on; it is optimistic.
4. **AntSeed prices.** The cheapest sellers are far below list prices (Opus 0.62 USD/M output vs 25 on OpenRouter; Luna 0.014 vs
   1.2) and are probably not what a buyer can rely on; the median over sellers is 6-170x higher on output price (Opus 9.7x, Luna 36x, DeepSeek 168x). Savings versus Opus are therefore
   very price-dependent. Prices change; this is one snapshot.
5. **Answers come from OpenRouter, not from AntSeed sellers.** Providers, token counts and reasoning behaviour can differ from what
   a given seller serves. One run per cell: model sampling noise is not measured.
6. **8000-token cap** truncated 8 of 360 answers (DeepSeek x4, GLM x2, Kimi x2; four AIME and four MMLU-Pro answers), which penalises
   reasoning-heavy models.
7. **Router model.** The success model was calibrated earlier on other tasks (a real held-out test for it, which is the intent);
   `deepseek-v4.1-flash` is not in its measured table and `kimi-k3` is partly extrapolated; the router does not know token
   appetite differences beyond its own estimate (DeepSeek emitted ~4x more tokens than Luna, which is why DeepSeek is not the
   cheapest at median prices).
8. OpenRouter reported `usage.cost` = 0 for the two OpenAI models (the account's credits are not charged for them); this does not
   affect results because costs are computed at AntSeed prices from token counts.
